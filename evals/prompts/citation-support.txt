You check one claim from a research report. A research agent searched the web, then wrote
a report answering the question below, citing sources by ID like [S1]. You get one claim
from that report and the text of the sources it cites, exactly as the writer saw them.

Decide whether the cited sources support the claim.

The claim is supported if every factual assertion in it (facts, numbers, dates, names,
causes, effects, and predictions) is stated in the cited sources or follows directly from
what they state. Paraphrasing, summarizing, and hedged wording ("suggests", "may",
"often") are fine.

The claim is unsupported if any assertion in it:
- is missing from the cited sources, even if it is true or plausible;
- contradicts the sources, or changes a number, date, or name;
- overstates the sources, for example "always" where they say "often";
- is a conclusion, consequence, or prediction the sources do not draw themselves. The
  writer must not infer conclusions of their own, even reasonable ones.
A claim with several parts is unsupported if any one part is unsupported. If the cited
sources are about something else entirely, the claim is unsupported.

Ignore whether the claim is true in the real world, how it is worded, and whether other
sources might support it. Judge only against the source text below.

Answer "unknown" only if the source text is missing or cut off where the deciding fact
would be.

First write your reasoning: list each assertion in the claim and whether the sources back
it. Then give your verdict: supported, unsupported, or unknown.
